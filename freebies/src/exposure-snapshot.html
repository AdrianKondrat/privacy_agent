Before you start

Six ways your data gets out.

Most privacy advice treats these as separate problems. They aren’t: a public record feeds a broker, a broker feeds a search result, a breach feeds a scam call. Scoring all six shows where your exposure actually comes from.

PathWho exposes itExample
StolenCriminals, through a company’s breach or malware on your deviceYour email and password in a breach

IndexedSearch engines and AI assistantsAll of the above, found by typing your name

Bonus · Ask the machines

Five AI prompts that need no private data.

AI assistants are useful for privacy work, as long as you don’t feed them what you’re trying to protect. Never paste passwords, ID numbers, bank details, or other people’s information. Use the [brackets] as placeholders and fill them in yourself afterwards.

1 · What does AI say about me?

Indexed · 2 min
What can you tell me about [full name] who lives in [town]? List any contact details, address, employer or family members you find, and the source of each.

2 · Decode a privacy policy

Shared · 3 min
Here is the privacy policy of [company]: [paste the policy, or its link]. In plain English: what does it collect, does it sell or share data for advertising, with which kinds of companies, and where exactly is the opt-out or delete-account option?

3 · Is this message a scam?

Stolen · 1 min
Is this message likely to be a scam? List the warning signs and what the real company would do instead: [paste the message with your name, numbers and links deleted]

4 · Draft a deletion request

Sold · Shared · 2 min
Write a short, polite email asking [company] to delete my account and personal data and stop using it for marketing, under [UK GDPR / EU GDPR / my US state’s privacy law]. Use placeholders for my name and account details. Don’t invent legal deadlines.

5 · Lock down a device

Shared · Posted · 5 min
Give me the exact menu steps, for [phone model and software version / app name], to turn off ad tracking, location history and public profile visibility. If a setting has moved in recent versions, say so.

AI can be wrong or out of date. Check any menu path or legal point against the official source before relying on it.

Bonus · Ten-minute settings

Stop the apps learning more.

Settings that cut what is shared from today. They don’t erase what was collected before. Menu names move between versions; search the settings for the key word if a path doesn’t match.

WhereWhat to switch
iPhoneSettings → Privacy & Security → Tracking → turn off “Allow Apps to Request to Track”.
AndroidSettings → Security & privacy → Privacy → Ads → Delete advertising ID. (Samsung: Security and privacy → More privacy settings → Ads.)
BrowserTurn on Global Privacy Control, an opt-out signal some US state laws require companies to honour. Built into Brave and DuckDuckGo, and in Firefox’s settings. Chrome, Safari and Edge need an extension. See globalprivacycontrol.org.
Facebook and InstagramAccounts Centre → look for “Activity from other businesses” (older accounts: “Your activity off Meta technologies”). It shows which businesses send Meta data about you, and limits how it is used for ads.
ChatGPTSettings → Data controls → turn off “Improve the model for everyone”. To ask OpenAI about personal data it holds, use privacy.openai.com.
GeminiGemini Apps Activity → turn off “Keep activity” (myactivity.google.com/product/gemini).
ClaudeSettings → Privacy → turn off the “Help improve Claude” setting.

These apply to future activity. Opting out of AI training applies to new chats; it does not remove past chats from models already trained.
