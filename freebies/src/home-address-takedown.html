Before you start

Why your address is out there, and why it comes back.

Your address doesn’t leak once. It flows: from a public register or a company you gave it to, into a data broker’s file, onto a people-search page, and into search results. Remove only the search result and the page is still there. Remove only the page and the broker relists it from the source.

1 · Source

Where it started

Electoral roll, company register, a retailer, a property record. Close or restrict this first so it stops feeding the rest.

2 · Copies

Who repackaged it

People-search sites and data brokers. Ask each one to remove or erase it. They may need asking again later.

3 · Search

Who points to it

Google, Bing and AI assistants. Ask them to drop results that show your address once the page itself is dealt with.

If you are worried about your safety

If someone is trying to find you to harm you, start with specialist help, not this guide. They can help with protections this guide can’t, such as anonymous registration.

UK: National Domestic Abuse Helpline, 0808 2000 247, free, 24 hours. US: National Domestic Violence Hotline, 1-800-799-7233. In an emergency, call 999 (UK), 112 (EU) or 911 (US).

A note on what is realistic

Some records exist by law and can’t be deleted, such as the full electoral register used for elections, or Land Registry titles in England and Wales. The goal is to cut the places where anyone can look you up for free, which is where most unwanted contact starts. In a 2024 Consumer Reports test in the US, people doing opt-outs by hand removed about 70% of their people-search listings within a week. Doing it yourself works.

Step 2 · Fix it · UK

The UK route.

The UK has fewer people-search sites than the US, but one big source feeds many of them: the open version of the electoral register, which anyone can buy.

1 · Opt out of the open register SOURCE

Follow gov.uk/electoral-register/opt-out-of-the-open-register, or contact your council’s electoral registration office (EONI in Northern Ireland). It does not affect your right to vote. Your choice carries forward each year, but you must opt out again when you move. Copies sold before you opt out can’t be recalled, so do the next steps too.

5 min

2 · Company director? Protect your home address SOURCE

If older Companies House filings show your home address, apply with form SR01 (search “Companies House SR01”) to remove it; the fee is £34 per document. If your company’s registered office is your home, move it first. New filings can use a service address instead.

15 min

3 · Ask each people-search site to remove you COPY

Use the site’s own removal or suppression page; 192.com, for example, has a removal request form. Look in the footer under “privacy” or “remove my details”. If there isn’t one, email its privacy contact using the request on page 6. Under UK GDPR you have a right to object to direct marketing and to ask for erasure; organisations normally have one month to respond.

10 min each

4 · Tell marketing lists to stop COPY

Register with the Mailing Preference Service (mpsonline.org.uk) for addressed junk mail; allow up to four months for full effect, and it doesn’t expire. Register with the Telephone Preference Service (tpsonline.org.uk) for sales calls; it takes up to 28 days. Both are free. Legitimate companies check them; scammers don’t.

5 min

5 · Remove what still shows in search SEARCH

Once a page has changed or gone, use Google’s “Remove outdated content” tool so the old snippet disappears. If a page still shows your contact details and the site won’t act, try Google’s “Results about you” (availability varies by country) or, if it isn’t offered where you live, Google’s legal removal request. Bing has its own content removal form.

5 min each

EU readers: the same rights apply under the GDPR. Electoral registers differ by country and are rarely sold; brokers and directory sites are the main copies. Use page 6 for each one.

Step 2 · Fix it · US

The US route.

In the US, people-search sites are a large industry and one company often runs many brands. Start with the tools that reach many brokers at once.

1 · California resident? Use DROP first COPY

The state’s free Delete Request and Opt-out Platform sends one deletion request to every registered data broker; since August 2026, registered brokers must check it at least every 45 days. It is free, at privacy.ca.gov/drop. You verify that you live in California yourself, and status updates can take up to 90 days.

15 min

2 · Use Google “Results about you” SEARCH

At myactivity.google.com/results-about-you. It finds Google results showing your phone number, email or home address, lets you request removal, and can alert you when new ones appear. It removes the result from Google, not the page itself, so pair it with step 3.

10 min

3 · Opt out of the people-search sites that show you COPY

Work from your list on page 3. Each site has an opt-out page, usually linked in its footer. Use an email address you don’t mind them having, and screenshot each confirmation. One opt-out can cover several sister sites, so check which network a site belongs to.

5–10 min each

4 · Stop pre-approved credit and insurance offers SOURCE

Use optoutprescreen.com, the official opt-out run for the credit bureaus. Online it lasts five years; a mailed form makes it permanent.

5 min

5 · Check your state’s privacy law COPY

A growing number of states give you the right to ask a company to delete your data and to stop selling it. Where yours does, a deletion request (page 6) carries legal weight beyond a voluntary opt-out.

—

Expect relisting

People-search sites rebuild profiles from fresh data. Recheck your list from page 3 in about three months, and again after you move house, change number or register to vote.

Step 2 · The request

When a site has no form, send this.

Send it to the site’s privacy or data protection email (look in its privacy policy). Give only the details they need to find your listing. Keep a copy with the date.

Subject: Request to erase my personal data and stop processing it

Hello,

Your site displays my personal data at [link to the page]. My name is [full name], and the listing shows my home address and [other details shown].

[UK/EU] Under Article 21 of the UK GDPR / GDPR, I object to the processing of my personal data, including for direct marketing. Under Article 17, I ask you to erase it, and under Article 19 to tell anyone you have disclosed it to.
[US] I request that you delete my personal information and stop selling or sharing it, under [your state] law where it applies, and under your own opt-out policy.

Please also tell me where you obtained my data.

Please confirm when this is done. I understand you normally have [one month (UK/EU) · 45 days (California)] to respond. If you need to confirm my identity, please ask only for what is necessary to match this listing.

Thank you,
[name] · [date]

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules on response times and identity checks change; the UK updated its rules in 2025. For the official UK guidance and templates, see the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk. If a UK or EU company ignores you after the deadline, you can complain to it, and then to the data protection regulator.
